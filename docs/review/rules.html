Tagging rules

The rules used to tag PRMS results and to review the tagging. They follow the CGIAR Standard for Tagging Climate-Related Work (10 November 2026), with taxonomy v6 and the choices made for the pilot of November 2026. Each section can be opened directly from the "More information" links in the review page.

How to review a result

  1. Read the title and the description.
  2. Every answer starts as extracted from the text. For each one: Agree, or Disagree. Disagree offers the right answer where there is a list, and an optional note. Each disagreement shows at once in the yellow box "What your answers give", with the PRMS marker, the levels and the marker answer.
  3. Check the tags first. Disagree opens the values to correct, a box to remove the tag, and an optional reason and note.
  4. Look at the words found but not tagged. Use "+ keep" if one should be a tag. Add a missing term: select the words in the text, or search the taxonomy, and say for which area it counts.
  5. Check the climate pathway.
  6. In each area card, check the climate function, the climate measure and the purpose. All questions are shown for both areas, even when nothing was extracted.
  7. Accept or reject the terms and synonyms proposed from the text. Propose your own if the taxonomy lacks one. Add a comment if needed.
  8. Save a draft or submit. A submitted review can still be changed until the second reviewer submits.

The reviewer never chooses a level or a marker answer. The page works them out from the answers, with the same table as the tagging script.

Climate information

  • Adaptation and mitigation are judged separately.
  • A text holds climate information for an area when at least one tag counts for that area.
  • A stand-alone term counts on its own (drought, early warning, greenhouse gas emissions).
  • A context-dependent term (irrigation, rice, training) counts only when the text links it to climate. That link is the anchor.
  • A term of both areas counts only for the area the text talks about.
  • A climate word inside a name (a programme, project, organisation, fund or event) is not climate information. A name cannot be an anchor.
  • "Climate" used as a label ("climate action", "for climate") is not climate information on its own.
  • Climate words used in another sense ("global warming potential" of a chemical, "climatically favourable areas") do not count.
  • Deforestation counts for mitigation only with carbon or emission wording.
  • CIS and CSA count as climate information services and climate-smart agriculture when the text spells them out.

"Training on drought-tolerant maize": drought is stand-alone and counts for adaptation; maize and training are anchored by drought.

Tags: count, do not count, not tagged

A word of the text can be in one of three states.

StateWhat it meansWhere it is on the page
Tag that countsThe term is tagged, and at least one area says stand-alone or anchored. It is climate information.Tags that count as climate information
Tag that does not countThe term is tagged, but every area says no anchor or not relevant. It is kept as a general tag. It is not climate information.Tags that do not count
Not taggedThe term search found the words in the text, but the AI or the code decided they are not a tag here (other meaning, part of a name, too generic).Words found in the text but not tagged

Example, the mechanized direct seeding report from Vietnam. Rice is tagged but does not count: for adaptation it has no anchor (pests and lodging are not climate hazards); for mitigation it is not relevant, because taxonomy v6 gives Rice no mitigation role, although the text speaks of low-emission rice. Mitigation is still found through other tags (Greenhouse gas emissions, Low-emission development, and Direct seeding and Fertilizer anchored by the carbon footprint passage), so the result is L2 for mitigation. Methodology is not tagged: the search found the word, and the AI did not keep it.

Rice not counting for mitigation is a gap in taxonomy v6, not a choice of the reviewer: systems that emit or store carbon (rice, cattle, livestock systems) have no mitigation role in v6, so the mitigation pathway has no "who or what". This is question B5 for the taxonomy team. The review page offers only the values the taxonomy allows, so Rice cannot be set to count for mitigation. A reviewer who thinks the taxonomy should give a term a role in an area writes it in the comment.

Checking a tag:

  • ✓ agree.
  • change: set another value for an area, another role in the text, or another status.
  • ✗ refuse, with a reason: different meaning in this text; part of a name; too generic; not about this work; other.

Columns of the tag table:

  • Found as: the words of the text where the term was found; the taxonomy term or synonym is highlighted.
  • Adaptation, Mitigation: the value for each area (next section), with the anchor passage when there is one.
  • Role in the text: climate reason (rationale); system or population (who or what is affected or targeted); intervention (what the work did); result (output, outcome, impact, or level not clear); none.
  • Planned or done: planned; carried out or reported; unclear. An aim or a recommendation is planned.

Stand-alone, anchored, no anchor, not relevant

Each tag has one value per area. The taxonomy decides which values are possible: a stand-alone term is stand-alone or not relevant; a context-dependent term is anchored, no anchor or not relevant.

ValueCounts?Meaning
stand-aloneyesThe term is climate-related by its own meaning.
anchoredyesA context-dependent term that the text links to climate: its own sentence is shown, or the note "anchored by the text".
no anchornoA context-dependent term with no link to climate in the text.
not relevantnoThe taxonomy gives the term no role in this area, or the text does not use it for this area.

The anchor is decided by the code, the same way every time (since script r17, 20 Nov 2026):

  • A context-dependent term counts for an area when its own sentence holds climate information of that area: a climate word (climate change, drought, flood, heat, emissions, carbon and similar), a stand-alone climate term, or a climate driver said to change ("erratic rainfall").
  • When the AI reading finds a climate measure for the area, every context-dependent term of that area in the text counts: the whole text describes climate work. The tag then shows "anchored by the text".
  • Otherwise only the terms in such a sentence count, so that a passing mention of climate does not make the whole text climate-related.
  • A name cannot be an anchor: climate words inside the name of a programme, project, fund or organisation are hidden first.

Climate measure

A climate measure is the work's own product or action with a specific climate function in this area. The AI reads the text and answers two questions with the exact words of the text; the code then applies the definition.

1. An action of this work. A measure can be: a product developed or tested (variety, technology, tool, platform, dataset, method, practice); a product or practice delivered, demonstrated, scaled or adopted; a training given by the work; an event organised by the work; a study that develops, tests or assesses responses; a policy, plan, strategy or recommendation the work contributed to or makes. A presentation, news item or brief about the work's own product takes the kind of that product.

2. Climate function, for adaptation and for mitigation:

ClassMeaningExample
specificThe text says the work's own product or action addresses a climate problem of the area, in the same sentence or in the work's own setting, or the product's name states its climate function."trials to promote farm diversification for climate resilience"; drought-tolerant variety; early warning system; greenhouse gas inventory
label onlyClimate words qualify a broad goal or frame, not the work's own product."towards climate-resilient food systems"; "for climate action"
context onlyClimate is background, a study variable, one item in a list, a finding or a name."climate change threatens production"; climate scenarios as model inputs
noneNo climate words of the area.

Measure = an action of this work from the list above and a specific climate function. Never a measure: a study that only describes a condition (trends, impacts, risks, vulnerability); speaking at an event of others; describing a programme, a partnership or the work of others; a general statement. A measure the work recommends or plans counts.

The AI reads each text twice, in two independent calls. A measure is kept only when both readings find one; when the readings differ, the result is flagged "the two readings differ: needs review" and the form shows what each reading said.

Answers in the review: agree; not a measure (with the reason); measure missed (paste the passage). When the AI found no measure: agree, no measure; or measure missed.

Purpose

Asked only when there is a climate measure. Climate is the main purpose, an additional purpose, or not stated. Four rules, the first that applies:

  1. Words. The sentence with the climate aim says main, primary, principal, central, core, fundamental or overarching: main. It says also, in addition, additional, secondary or co-benefit for the climate aim: additional.
  2. Title. The title presents the result by its climate function and names no non-climate aim or benefit: main. The title names the climate function together with a non-climate aim or benefit (income, yield, productivity, nutrition, gender, restoration, peace, labour, water saving, market access), or presents the result only by a non-climate aim: additional. A label, a finding about climate or a name in the title is not a climate function: go to rule 3.
  3. List. A sentence of the description names the climate benefit together with non-climate benefits or aims of the result: additional.
  4. Otherwise: not stated.

Main is never given only because the text describes a climate problem, a response and an intention.

"Diversified rice based systems for climate resilience expanded in Cambodia": rule 2, main. "Direct Seeders: Reducing Labor, Ensuring Timely Field Operations, and Mitigating End-of-Season Drought": rule 2, additional.

"The programme's primary objective is to raise rural incomes. As an additional objective, it delivers heat-stress advisories." Measure: the heat-stress advisories. Purpose: additional.

Levels L0 to L3

Each area gets a level. The level is a step in the comparison with the marker. It is not a score of the work.

LevelThe text
L0holds no climate information for the area.
L1mentions climate (a hazard, climate change) but describes no climate measure.
L2describes a climate measure; climate is an additional purpose, or the purpose is not stated.
L3describes a climate measure, and climate is the main purpose.

The level comes from three answers: does a tag count, is there a climate measure, what is the purpose.

PRMS climate marker

The marker recorded in PRMS for the result, on the OECD-DAC scale:

  • (2) Principal: climate is fundamental to the design or motivation of the work.
  • (1) Significant: climate is an important but additional purpose.
  • (0) Not targeted: the work does not target climate.
  • Not provided: no marker. A missing marker is never read as 0.

PRMS has one marker for adaptation and mitigation together. It is compared with the stronger of the two areas. Only the text is compared: the evidence for a marker may sit in other documents. The marker is never changed.

  • aligned: the text is consistent with the marker. For Not targeted, it means only that no conflict is apparent in the text.
  • marker higher: the marker may not be reflected in the text.
  • marker lower: the text describes more than the marker says.
  • cannot determine: the text gives too little to judge (Rule 9 of the standard). The reason follows the answer.
  • no comparison: PRMS records no marker, or the text is empty.

An answer is a signal for review. It is not a finding that the marker is wrong.

Climate pathway

The climate story of the text, in five parts, for each area. A missing part means the text does not describe it. It does not mean the work lacks it.

  • Climate reason: the hazard or climate problem.
  • Where: from the Countries field in PRMS; from the text when the field is empty.
  • Who or what: the system or population affected or targeted. An organisation that delivers the work is not the "who".
  • What was done: the intervention.
  • What came out: the result, as output, outcome or impact.

Two links are recorded when the text gives them: from the climate reason to what was done, and from what was done to what came out (stated, or clear from context). Each part is planned, or carried out or reported. To correct a part, choose Disagree under the pathway and write which part is wrong.

Words found in the text but not tagged

Before the AI reads a text, a search marks every word that matches a taxonomy term or synonym. The AI then keeps some of these words as tags and leaves the others out. The code can also refuse a tag that fails a check. This section lists the words that were marked and then left out, with where they are in the text and why.

Reason shownWhat happened
The AI decided this term does not apply hereThe AI read the text and left the word out without a more specific reason.
Another meaning in this textThe word is used in another sense ("model" as an example to follow, not a computer model).
Part of a nameThe word is inside the name of a programme, project, organisation, event or publication.
Too generalThe word says nothing specific about the work ("works with sweet potato").
A narrower term is already taggedTwo terms match the same words; only the narrower one is kept ("Local government" rather than "Public institution").
Words not in the textThe AI quoted words that are not in the text word for word ("public sectors" when the text says something else). Every quote must be in the text, so the code refused the tag.
Area not addressedThe term belongs to one area in the taxonomy and the text does not talk about that area.

A word left out is not wrong in itself: most are general words that say nothing about climate. Use "+ keep" when one should be a tag, then say for which area it counts.

AI taxonomy findings

What the text shows about the taxonomy. Four kinds:

  • a climate phrase with no matching term;
  • a term matched in a different sense;
  • a term that may sit in the wrong class;
  • a new kind of anchor.

Accept: the taxonomy team should look at it. Reject: not a real gap. Later: no view (the default).

Proposing a term or a synonym

A synonym is another wording of an existing term. A new term is a concept the taxonomy lacks. Give the label, the facet, the existing term for a synonym, and a note. A person decides. Nothing enters the taxonomy by itself.

Outcome of the review

Worked out from the answers:

  • wrong: a level or the marker answer differs from the AI's;
  • minor gap: only tags, terms, the pathway or the purpose differ;
  • captured well: nothing differs.

Taxonomy findings and proposals do not change the outcome.

Two reviewers

  • Each result is reviewed by two people.
  • A reviewer sees the other review only when both are submitted.
  • Once both are submitted, the two reviews are locked.
  • The two reviewers agree when their levels and marker answer match. Lists: to review, waiting for the second review, done and agree, done and disagree.

Flags

Reasons for a person to look at the result first. A flag is not an error.

FlagMeaning
the two readings differ: needs reviewThe two independent AI readings of the text disagree on what the work did, the climate function, the measure or the purpose. The area keeps only what both support; the form shows what each reading said.
(area) intervention tag but no measure describedA tag counts as an intervention for the area, but no climate measure was found.
answers adjusted by codeThe code changed or cancelled an answer: a quote not in the text, a role the taxonomy does not allow, an area taken from the text, terms anchored by the text, a climate function or purpose whose words do not hold.

very short textToo little text to judge much.
