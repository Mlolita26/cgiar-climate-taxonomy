Tagging review: how to review, definitions and rules

This page follows the CGIAR Standard for Tagging Climate-Related Work, as revised on 24 and 25 November 2026, with taxonomy v6. The standard is the only source of the rules. The numbers, the titles and the words of the rules are the standard's. This page applies to batches tagged with that version of the rules.

1. How to review a text

How to review a text

You check what the tagging found in one text. A text is a written record: a PRMS result (its title and description), a part of a plan, an abstract. Use only what the text says. Do not add what you know from elsewhere, and do not judge the work.

The form follows the four questions of the standard. Every answer is already filled in from the tagging. For each one, choose Agree, or choose Disagree and fix it. A note is always optional.

A word in pink and underlined has an explanation: point at it, or tap it.

  1. Question 1, the terms. Read the text on the left. On the right there are two tables.
    • Terms that count. They say that the text holds climate-related information. A term counts when it is a stand-alone term, or when it is anchored.
    • Terms that do not count. They are in the text, but they do not say that it is about climate. The reason is on each line: most often the text gives no climate anchor. Words that were found and not tagged are in the same table.
    If a line is wrong, choose Disagree and fix it. For a word that was not tagged, click + keep. If a taxonomy term is missing, add it under Add a term. The pink box gives the answer to question 1.
  2. Question 2, the climate pathway. The climate story of the text, part by part: climate rationale, geography, system or population, intervention, result, and the relationships between them. Agree with each part, or disagree and say what is wrong in your own words.
  3. Question 3, purpose and verdict. One card for adaptation and one for mitigation. First: does the text show a purpose? Then: which marker does the text support? That is the verdict. If you disagree, pick the right answer in the list.
  4. Question 4, the taxonomy. Words of the text that the taxonomy does not have yet. Say yes or no to each one, and add your own. This does not change the result for the text.
  5. Look at the yellow box, then submit. It shows the result for the text: the verdicts and the comparison with the assigned marker, as the tagging gives them and as your answers give them. Below, it lists the points where you disagree.

Each text is reviewed by two people. You do not see the other person's answers until both of you have submitted.

2. Definitions

From section 2 of the standard.

Text
The written record being tagged, such as a PRMS result (title and description), a project or activity description, a theory of change, or a publication abstract. Each text is tagged on its own. The tagger uses only what the text says and does not add outside knowledge.
Work
What the text describes: an activity, a project, a result, or another element of a climate pathway.
Climate-related information
What a text says that concerns adaptation or mitigation: a taxonomy term that is stand-alone, or a context-dependent term with a climate anchor in the text. A text does not need a complete pathway: a hazard alone is climate-related information.
Climate adaptation
Responses to actual or expected climate conditions and their effects, for example reducing harm or vulnerability associated with drought or extreme heat.
Climate mitigation
Reducing greenhouse gas emissions, or maintaining or increasing the removal or storage of greenhouse gases.
Taxonomy, tagging, tag
The CGIAR climate taxonomy is a structured vocabulary of agreed concepts relating to adaptation and mitigation. Tagging means identifying a concept in a text and attaching the corresponding taxonomy term, with the passage that supports the match. A tag describes information in the text; it is not a judgment about the objectives or the quality of the work.
Stand-alone term
A term whose meaning is already climate-related, such as drought, climate-resilient variety or greenhouse gas inventory. It signals climate-related information without another concept.
Context-dependent term
A term that is also used outside climate work, such as irrigation, yield, maize, resilience, agroforestry or rainfall. Its relevance to adaptation or mitigation depends on the surrounding text.
Climate anchor
The information in the text that makes a context-dependent term relevant to adaptation or mitigation: a climate hazard, an emission source, a greenhouse gas or another clearly climate-related concept. The relationship may be stated or be reasonably clear from the context. A merely incidental or unrelated climate mention does not anchor every term in the text.
Climate pathway
It connects a climate rationale (why action is needed), an intervention (what is done) and a result (what is produced or changes). Five components are recorded: climate rationale, geography, system or population, intervention and result.
Climate rationale
The climate hazard or emission source the work responds to. Adaptation: climate change, a climate hazard or a climate-related vulnerability, such as drought, flooding or heat stress. Mitigation: greenhouse gas emissions, an emission source, or carbon removal or storage, such as methane from cattle.
Intervention
An action that the work undertakes or delivers, such as an innovation, a technology, practice, product, service, policy, research activity, research method, capacity-building such as training, capacity sharing, partnership or stakeholder engagement activity.
Result
An output (a product or service delivered), an outcome (a change in behaviour, capacity, performance or conditions) or an impact (a longer-term change).
Purpose
What the text says an intervention or the work is for, the change it is meant to bring about. A purpose counts only if it is written in the text and the text links it to an intervention. It is an adaptation purpose, a mitigation purpose or a non-climate purpose.
Adaptation purpose
One of the following, in relation to current or expected climate change, including climate variability: reducing the vulnerability of people or natural systems; strengthening their resilience; enhancing their adaptive capacity; reducing their exposure to climate hazards. Resilience or adaptive capacity count only when the text links it to a climate rationale. "Resilient livelihoods" with no climate reference is not an adaptation purpose.
Mitigation purpose
Reducing or limiting greenhouse gas emissions; protecting or enhancing carbon sinks and reservoirs; integrating climate change concerns into development objectives through institution building, capacity development, policy or regulation, or research; supporting a country's efforts to meet its obligations under the UNFCCC.
Non-climate purpose
Any purpose that is neither, for example higher yields, higher income, better nutrition, reduced poverty.
National or subnational climate plan
A plan, strategy or policy adopted by a national government or by a government at a lower level, whose stated subject is climate adaptation or mitigation: a National Adaptation Plan (NAP), a National Adaptation Programme of Action (NAPA), a Nationally Appropriate Mitigation Action (NAMA), a Nationally Determined Contribution (NDC), a county or city climate action plan.
OECD-DAC climate marker
Principal (2): the climate purpose is fundamental to the work. Significant (1): it is explicit but not the fundamental purpose. Not targeted (0): the work has been examined and does not target that purpose. A missing marker is not the same as 0.
Assigned marker
The OECD-DAC climate marker recorded for the work by the person reporting, for example in PRMS or in project records. A missing marker means the work was not assessed and is never treated as 0. PRMS records one climate marker for adaptation and mitigation together.
Verdict
The outcome of applying Rules 7 and 8 to a text, recorded separately for adaptation and for mitigation: Not targeted (0), Significant (1), Principal (2), or Cannot be determined. The first three are the markers that the text supports. Cannot be determined means that the text alone does not allow any of them to be assigned with confidence, so a person must decide using additional documents.
3. The tagging rules

Each rule is given with the number and the title it has in the standard.

What climate-related information does the text contain?

Rule 1. Identify adaptation and mitigation information separately

For each text, identify whether its information concerns adaptation, mitigation, both or neither, using each taxonomy term's area classification and the context in which the term is used. A term classified as relevant to both areas may support one area, both or neither in a particular text. Do not attribute a function only because it is a generally known possible benefit of the intervention.

Agroforestry presented as protecting crops from heat contributes adaptation information; presented as storing carbon, mitigation information. If both functions are described, both areas are recorded.

Rule 2. Use stand-alone terms to identify climate-related information

A stand-alone term provides climate-related information on its own, without a climate anchor in the surrounding text. Once the term is matched according to its definition, record it for adaptation, mitigation or both as supported by the taxonomy and the text. If the classification cannot be established, record it as "unclear": the classification is not forced.

Stand-alone terms are not confused with similar, more general terms: resilience is not climate resilience, and manure management is not low-emission manure management.

"Climate action" can concern adaptation, mitigation or both. If the text does not show which, its area is unclear.

Rule 3. Use climate anchors to assess context-dependent terms

A context-dependent term contributes adaptation or mitigation information when it has a climate anchor in the text. Anchors that are explicit or reasonably clear from the surrounding context are accepted, also across sentences of the same description. The text need not contain words such as "because of" or "in response to". When no climate anchor is found, the tag is kept as a general tag and does not count as adaptation or mitigation information.

"The district faces recurring drought. The project installed irrigation systems": irrigation is anchored for adaptation. A crop mentioned only as the subject of a project is not adaptation-related, and an unrelated reference to drought does not make every intervention of a long text an adaptation intervention.

What climate pathway does the text describe?

Rule 4. Identify climate pathway components

For adaptation and mitigation separately, identify the five components: climate rationale, geography, system or population, intervention and result. Record each component that the text describes; a component that the text does not provide is "not described". A general reference still counts: "climate change" is a general adaptation rationale. No scoring of specific against non-specific is made.

ComponentAdaptationMitigation
Climate rationaleClimate change, a climate hazard or a climate-related vulnerabilityGreenhouse gas emissions, an emission source, or carbon removal or storage
GeographyThe place or area associated with the climate issue or the work
System or populationCrops, livestock, ecosystems, people or other systems exposed or vulnerable to the climate issueActivities, systems or populations that emit greenhouse gases, or systems that remove or store carbon
InterventionAn action, measure, research product or service relevant to adaptationThe same, relevant to mitigation
ResultAn adaptation-related output, outcome or impact described by the textA mitigation-related output, outcome or impact

Geography is not, by itself, proof that work addresses a climate problem. An organisation delivering an intervention is not automatically a population exposed to a climate hazard.

Rule 5. Describe the relationships between pathway components supported by the text

Where the text provides a connection, record the relationship between the climate rationale and the intervention, and between the intervention and the result. A relationship may be stated or reasonably clear from the context. Components that appear in the same text are not necessarily connected. The absence of a stated relationship means that the text does not describe it, not that it is absent from the work.

Rule 6. Classify interventions and results, and distinguish intended from realized information

For each intervention, keep its type as defined in the taxonomy. For each result, identify whether it is an output, an outcome or an impact; where the level cannot be determined, no category is forced. Distinguish intended (planned, expected) interventions and results from realized ones (carried out, achieved). An expected benefit is not presented as an achieved result.

"will develop a drought advisory service": an intended intervention. "farmers reported fewer drought-related losses": a realized result.

Is the information reported consistent with the assigned climate marker?

Rule 7. Identify the climate purpose (if any)

Applied separately for adaptation and for mitigation. The text has an adaptation or a mitigation purpose if it shows it in one of four ways. In ways 1 to 3, the text connects an intervention to the climate rationale as something the intervention addresses. In way 4, the text connects the work to a named national or subnational climate plan.

WayThe textExample
1states what the intervention or work is for"Farmers adopted mulching to retain soil moisture during dry spells."
2describes the intervention or work by the climate rationale"drought-tolerant maize", "flood early warning system", "methane-reducing feed additive"
3reports a result of the intervention in relation to the climate rationale"fewer livestock deaths during the 2024 drought", "reduced methane emissions per litre of milk"
4states that the work arises from, implements or contributes to a named national or subnational climate planAn adaptation plan (NAP, NAPA) shows an adaptation purpose; a mitigation plan (NAMA) a mitigation purpose. For a plan that covers both (NDC), the part the text names; if it names none, ways 1 to 3 decide the area.

The following show no adaptation or mitigation purpose:

  • the absence of a climate rationale, unless way 4 applies;
  • a climate rationale mentioned without a connection to an intervention ("Climate change threatens farming in the region. The project trained 200 farmers in bookkeeping.");
  • a general label, such as "climate-smart", "climate action" or "resilient", that does not name a climate rationale;
  • a general claim, such as "climate benefits" or "contributes to climate resilience", that does not name a climate rationale;
  • a reference to a plan, strategy, policy or priority whose subject is not climate change adaptation or mitigation.

Rule 8. Assign a climate marker based on text

Applied separately for adaptation and for mitigation. The rules are applied in this order; the first one that applies gives the verdict.

VerdictThe text supports this marker whenExample
Not targeted (0)N1. Rule 7 found no adaptation or mitigation purpose."Climate change threatens farming in the region. The project trained 200 farmers in bookkeeping."
Principal (2)P1. The purpose is determined by an existing, named national or subnational climate plan (way 4)."Activities to implement the county climate adaptation plan."
P2. The work produces or supports the development of a national or subnational climate plan, strategy or policy, or builds capacity, tools or finance to include climate change in policy, planning or investment."Supported the ministry to develop its greenhouse gas inventory."
P3. Adaptation, any type of intervention: the purpose is shown through way 1 or way 2, and it is not limited to one component of work whose stated purpose is something else."Released two bean varieties in Malawi that combine high iron content with heat tolerance, helping smallholder farmers maintain yields as temperatures rise."
P4. Mitigation, any type of intervention: the same."Farmers adopted alternate wetting and drying in rice fields to reduce methane emissions."
Significant (1)S1. The purpose concerns only one piece of the work: the text states that the work is for something else, and gives the purpose only for one of its interventions, or for one characteristic of an intervention."The project aims to raise dairy incomes. It trained farmers in feeding practices, including a feed additive that reduces methane emissions."
S2. The purpose is shown only by reporting a result of the intervention in relation to the rationale (way 3)."Trained 1,200 dairy farmers on improved feeding, increasing milk yields by 18% and reducing methane emissions per litre of milk."
Cannot be determinedNo rule above applies with confidence, so a person decides using additional documents: the text does not describe what the work did; the text can be read in two ways that lead to different verdicts; or it names a climate plan that covers both areas without saying which part, and ways 1 to 3 do not show which."Output 3.2: Kenya dairy." "Contributed to Ethiopia's NDC."

Check when both adaptation and mitigation are Principal. Principal for both is accepted only if each rests on different quoted words. If only one has its own quoted words, it keeps Principal and the rules are applied again to the other without those words. If both rest only on the same words, the verdict for both is Cannot be determined.

Rule 9. Compare the text with the expectations for the assigned marker, record alignment/misalignment

For adaptation and for mitigation, the record holds: the verdict; the way that showed the purpose, with the words quoted; the quoted climate rationale; the rule that decided, with the words it rests on; any flag for human review. The verdict is then compared with the assigned marker.

Verdict compared with the assigned markerComparison result
The same markerConsistent
The assigned marker is higherThe text supports a lower marker
The assigned marker is lowerThe text supports a higher marker
No marker assignedMarker missing (not treated as 0)
Cannot be determinedNot compared; a person decides using additional documents

The comparison result is a signal for review. It does not replace the assigned marker or show that it is wrong: the evidence for a marker may sit in other documents. When the assigned marker and the verdict are both 0, Consistent means only that this text shows no conflict.

What gaps or errors in the taxonomy does tagging reveal?

Rule 10. Identify possible gaps and errors

FindingWhat it means
Unmatched climate phraseThe text expresses a relevant climate concept, and no approved term or synonym represents it.
Doubtful matchA term or synonym matches the wording but has a different meaning in context.
Possible misclassificationThe use of a term suggests reviewing its classification as stand-alone or context-dependent, its area, or its concept type.
Unrecorded type of climate anchorThe text shows a reasonable anchor for a context-dependent term that the taxonomy's guidance does not describe yet.

An unfamiliar phrase is not necessarily a missing concept. A single example does not establish that a term is wrongly classified.

Rule 11. Distinguish missing synonyms from missing concepts

Propose a synonym when a phrase has substantially the same meaning as an existing term and would not introduce misleading matches. Propose a new term when the phrase refers to a distinct concept. A general expression is not added as a synonym of a climate term simply because it appears in a climate document: vulnerability is not necessarily a synonym of climate vulnerability.

Rule 12. Record proposed changes for review

For every finding, keep the type of issue, the phrase and passage, the existing term concerned, and the proposed action with a brief reason. The taxonomy reviewers decide. Unapproved proposals do not silently become approved taxonomy terms or definitions.

4. Reading the page

What the colours, the values and the boxes of the review page mean. These are not tagging rules.

Colours in the text

  • Yellow: a tag that counts as climate-related information (a stand-alone term, or a context-dependent term with a climate anchor).
  • Grey: a tag that does not count (a context-dependent term with no climate anchor, or a term with an unclear area).
  • Blue: a term added by you.
  • Purple, dotted: a proposed tag, words that no taxonomy term covers (decided under question 4).

Only the taxonomy term or synonym is coloured, at its place in the text. Click a colour to find its line on the right. Words that were found and not tagged are not coloured.

Terms that do not count

One table holds everything that is in the text and does not count as climate-related information. The column "Why it does not count" gives the reason.

  • A tag with no climate anchor. The term is in the text with its taxonomy meaning (maize is maize), but the text does not link it to climate. It stays a general tag.
  • A word that was not tagged. A search found the word, and it was left out because the words mean something else here. The line starts with "Not tagged".

One case is listed apart, under the tables: a broader term left out because a more precise term is tagged on the same words. In "greenhouse gas emissions" the tag is Greenhouse gas emissions, not also Greenhouse gas. The words are tagged, so this is not a term that does not count. Open the line to see these terms; + keep adds one.

Point at a term to read its taxonomy definition. If a tag should count, choose Disagree and change its value. If a word that was not tagged should be a tag, click + keep.

Add a term

Use it when the text holds a taxonomy term that was not tagged. Search the taxonomy by term or synonym, or select the words in the text; click + add; then tick the area for which the term counts. If the words are not in the taxonomy, propose them as a new term or as a synonym: the proposal goes to question 4.

Flags ("To look at first")

Points to look at first. A flag is not an error. The line appears only when a text has flags. The main ones:

  • the text was read twice and the two readings lead to different verdicts;
  • a verdict cannot be determined, with the reason;
  • adaptation or mitigation rests on climate anchors only;
  • climate-related information with an unclear area;
  • Principal for both areas;
  • answers adjusted by code: a check changed an answer of the tagging, for example a quote that was not in the text;
  • translated, or a very short text;
  • the countries recorded in PRMS differ from the countries named in the text.

The result for this text, and what your review says

The yellow box at the end shows, for the tagging and for your answers: the verdict for adaptation, the verdict for mitigation, the assigned marker and the comparison. Under each verdict a few words say why. You do not fill in these values: the page works them out from your answers. Below, it lists the points where you disagree.

The last line says what your review means for the tagging of this text:

  • right: you agree with everything;
  • nearly right: you corrected tags, the pathway or the purpose, and the verdicts and the comparison stay the same;
  • wrong: with your answers a verdict or the comparison changes.

Two reviewers

Each text is reviewed by two people, without seeing each other's answers. When both have submitted, the page shows the two reviews side by side. The reviewers agree when they end with the same verdicts and the same comparison result.
